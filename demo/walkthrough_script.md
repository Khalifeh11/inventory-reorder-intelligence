## 0:00–0:30  Problem recap (30 seconds)

> "This is the proof of concept for the third ZAKA deliverable. The client is Atlas Paints & Tools — a family-owned Lebanese paint manufacturer and hardware wholesaler. From the first-deliverable readiness assessment and second-deliverable roadmap, we selected **Inventory Reorder Intelligence** as the initiative to build — it scored highest on combined impact and feasibility. Atlas's real pain: hardware imports take two months to arrive, and today nobody reorders until a SKU hits zero. That means two-month stockouts on the primary revenue category, plus deadstock piling up on slow-movers."

[Show: the prioritization matrix from the 2nd deliverable / Slide 18]

---

## 0:30–1:00  Approach in 30 seconds

> "The approach is deliberately simple. For each SKU, we learn its normal monthly rhythm from ten years of history, project it over the supplier lead time, and tell the GM to order when stock drops below what he'll sell during the wait plus a buffer — the noisier the demand or the longer the wait, the bigger the buffer. No ML; a classical reorder-point formula. On top of that, a bounded LLM layer does the analyst's job — it writes a weekly briefing that triages the batch, telling the GM which few orders actually matter, what to defer, and where to consolidate POs by supplier, plus a plain-language 'why' on each one. It's grounding-checked so it can't invent a number, and it falls back to an offline template, so the system still works when the internet drops. Everything runs on Atlas's on-prem server. No cloud."

[Show: the Data Flow diagram from the proposal (Slide 13 of the PPTX) or the technical-overview architecture box]

## 3:00–4:00  Evaluation (1 minute)

> "Backtest on the last twelve months of synthetic data: median forecast MAPE is about twelve percent on paint, which is good, and about seventy percent on hardware, which looks bad — but it's honest. Hardware is low-volume, lumpy, two-month lead time. A high MAPE there is expected. That's exactly why the design uses classical reorder-point logic instead of chasing forecast accuracy: the safety-stock term, sized to service level, absorbs the forecast error. If the client later wants tighter numbers on hardware, Croston-style intermittent-demand models are the queued Phase 2.5 upgrade."

[Show: the notebook's MAPE histogram and the category breakdown]

> "And critically — the PoC's simulated reactive-ordering baseline shows stockouts lasting the full sixty-day lead time, which matches the real-world pain Atlas described. The recommendations would have prevented most of them."

## Recording tips

- Use a **clean screen recording tool** (QuickTime or OBS). Target 1920×1080, 30 fps, voice-over at normal pace.
- **Pre-warm the app** before recording: run `streamlit run app.py` once and click into the SKU drilldown to cache the LLM explanation if you have the API key set. Avoid awkward loading pauses in the demo.
- **Pre-pick the SKU** you'll click in the drilldown — ideally one marked high-risk with `should_reorder = True` (e.g. `H0022 — Cordless Drill 12V` as of 2026-03-31 in the synthetic data).
- Keep the proposal PPTX open in a second window so you can quickly flip to the prioritization matrix and data-flow slides.
- Final cut: aim for **4:45–5:15** so there's breathing room.
